How “points above replacement” (PAR) is calculated

PAR = the points a traded player scored while started for the manager who received him minus what a replacement-level player at his position would have scored over the same number of starts: PAR = pts − replacement PPG × games started. A player who never started is 0; one who started but scored below replacement is negative.

Replacement level is set per season and position: the points per game of the player ranked at a cutoff. The cutoff = the average number of that position managers actually started each week (measured from the real lineups, so flex and superflex slots are counted automatically) + bench depth (half a player per team at QB, TE, K and DEF; one per team at RB and WR) × the number of teams. Example: 10 teams, one QB slot → about 10 starters + 5 = QB15.

Every NFL player at the position is ranked by points per game, using this league’s own scoring for that season (rebuilt from Sleeper’s raw weekly stats; it matches Sleeper’s weekly scores exactly), over completed regular-season weeks, counting only players who played at least 40% of them. The current season’s cutoffs are based on the weeks played so far and will move. Hover a cell for the details.

PAR/G divides a trade’s net PAR by the number of weeks it was live (the most weeks any player in it was on his new roster, started or benched), so a week-2 trade and a week-12 trade can be compared on equal terms. An owner’s PAR/G is their total net PAR divided by the total live weeks of their trades.

Every player value is normalized to a 0–100 scale within its own season, where 100 = the single most valuable player charted that season — this keeps seasons comparable even though the underlying value source changes over time. 2021–2022 values come from CBS Sports' single-QB redraft trade chart; 2023–2025 use CBS's 2QB/superflex chart (this league went superflex in 2023); both are matched to the week closest to each trade, using the nearest earlier chart when that exact week wasn't published. Starting in 2026, values come from a live 12-team, PPR, 2QB redraft trade-value feed instead of CBS. Team defenses and a handful of players never listed on a chart that season get a small flat placeholder (2–3 on the normalized scale) rather than a real market value. Trades where one side gave nothing (a player-for-FAAB trade, since FAAB isn't captured as an asset) are excluded entirely. The Fantasy Pts columns are a separate, real measurement from actual games: for each traded player, points earned in the acquiring manager's starting lineup, counting from the first game played after the trade (the week after, or the trade week itself when the trade was made before that week's Sunday games — 2026 onward) and stopping the moment that manager trades or drops them again; weeks not yet played aren't counted; benched weeks score 0 and don't end the count, but leaving the roster does. If the acquiring manager's team missed the playoffs that season, counting also stops at the end of the regular season (week 15 for 2021–2025, week 14 starting 2026) even if they kept the player — points scored once a non-playoff team's season is over don't count.
