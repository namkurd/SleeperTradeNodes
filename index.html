How “lineup Δ” is calculated

For every week after a trade, take the roster each manager really had and find the best lineup it could have started: every player on the roster (bench included) is scored with his real points that week, and this season’s lineup slots (QB, 2 RB, TE, WR/flex and superflex slots, K, DEF as the league had them) are filled to maximise the total. Then undo the trade — take away the players the manager received, put back the players he gave up — and find the best lineup again. Lineup Δ = best lineup with the trade − best lineup without it, summed over the weeks the trade was live.

Because both versions use the same roster otherwise (same waiver pickups, drops and other trades), the number already prices in the depth a manager traded away and the depth he could plug in: a spare WR who would have filled the flex counts as lost if he was sent away, and a surplus starter who would have sat on the bench adds nothing. Nothing is estimated — every number is a real score from that week. Points are this league’s own scoring (rebuilt from Sleeper’s raw weekly stats; matches Sleeper’s scores exactly).

Weeks counted: from the first week the trade counts (the week after it) until the manager no longer rosters anyone he received, or the end of week 17 (end of the regular season if his team missed the playoffs). Only completed weeks count, so the current season grows week by week. The best lineup is the hindsight-optimal one for both versions, so it measures what the roster could have produced, not what was actually started.

The two sides don’t cancel out. A trade can improve both lineups (each manager fills a need) or hurt both. “Net Δ” is A’s gain minus B’s. Δ/G divides by the weeks the trade was live so a week-2 trade and a week-12 trade can be compared; an owner’s Δ/G is their total gain divided by their total live weeks.

Outcome icons use the manager’s own lineup Δ (averaging less than ±3 points per week = Meh) together with the sign of the trade-chart value. Every player value is normalized to a 0–100 scale within its own season, where 100 = the single most valuable player charted that season — this keeps seasons comparable even though the underlying value source changes over time. 2021–2022 values come from CBS Sports' single-QB redraft trade chart; 2023–2025 use CBS's 2QB/superflex chart (this league went superflex in 2023); both are matched to the week closest to each trade, using the nearest earlier chart when that exact week wasn't published. Starting in 2026, values come from a live 12-team, PPR, 2QB redraft trade-value feed instead of CBS. Team defenses and a handful of players never listed on a chart that season get a small flat placeholder (2–3 on the normalized scale) rather than a real market value. Trades where one side gave nothing (a player-for-FAAB trade, since FAAB isn't captured as an asset) are excluded entirely. The Fantasy Pts columns are a separate, real measurement from actual games: for each traded player, points earned in the acquiring manager's starting lineup, counting from the first game played after the trade (the week after, or the trade week itself when the trade was made before that week's Sunday games — 2026 onward) and stopping the moment that manager trades or drops them again; weeks not yet played aren't counted; benched weeks score 0 and don't end the count, but leaving the roster does. If the acquiring manager's team missed the playoffs that season, counting also stops at the end of the regular season (week 15 for 2021–2025, week 14 starting 2026) even if they kept the player — points scored once a non-playoff team's season is over don't count.
